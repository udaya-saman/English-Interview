Workplace culture & etiquette

  • Be punctual and ready to start on time.
  • Communication may sometimes be direct. A direct question or correction is not automatically a personal insult.
  • Listen carefully and ask when an instruction is unclear.
  • Show initiative, but do not take unauthorized or unsafe actions.
  • Keep politics, religious arguments and stereotypes out of professional interview answers unless the interviewer specifically asks a neutral question about workplace practice.
  • Use calm, respectful communication even when another person is direct or frustrated.
About “Dugri”: It is useful to understand that some Israeli communication can be straightforward and direct. Treat this as a communication tendency, not a rule about every Israeli person.

Work week, rest and overtime — learn the principle, not a slogan

  • The statutory framework includes a 42-hour regular work week for many employees, with specific rules and sector exceptions.
  • Overtime and work during weekly rest can attract additional pay, but the exact calculation depends on the law, work pattern, sector and circumstances.
  • Weekly rest is generally at least 36 consecutive hours, with rules differing for Jewish and non-Jewish employees regarding the chosen rest day.
  • Do not tell an interviewer “I can work unlimited hours.” A professional answer is that you are flexible within lawful working conditions, your contract and the roster.
This section is general information, not legal advice. Always check the current law, your permit, your employment agreement and applicable sector rules.

Shabbat & holidays

  • Shabbat runs from Friday evening into Saturday evening.
  • Jewish festivals and Independence Day have legal holiday rules; exact dates move each year with the Hebrew calendar.
  • Work arrangements vary by employer, sector, religious affiliation and applicable permits.
  • Non-Jewish employees have rules concerning their weekly rest day and holidays; the applicable choice should be handled according to the law and employment arrangements.

Foreign-worker rights

  • Foreign workers generally have basic employment protections applicable to workers in Israel, plus specific protections.
  • Official guidance covers written employment agreements, private health insurance and suitable accommodation.
  • Wage, rest-day, holiday, overtime, deductions and other rights depend on current law, sector rules, permits and contractual terms.
  • Do not sign or agree to something you do not understand. Ask for an explanation or appropriate assistance.
Professional interview line: “I will follow my legal visa, employment agreement, workplace procedures and Israeli law. If I do not understand a rule, I will ask for clarification.”

Commerce & Services sector

The Population and Immigration Authority has a permit framework for employers in the commerce and services sector, including retail and wholesale commerce and other listed service groups. A worker’s actual status depends on the employer’s permit, the worker’s visa/permit and the current procedure.

Do not memorize a fixed “maximum stay” or a fixed work schedule for every candidate. Use the actual legal documents and current official instructions.

Safety & emergency awareness

  • Follow official Home Front Command instructions and your employer’s safety procedures.
  • If you hear an alert, protect yourself and go to the appropriate protected place according to current official instructions.
  • Do not improvise, take unnecessary risks or film an emergency instead of getting to safety.
  • In a job interview, keep your answer factual: stay calm, follow official instructions and cooperate with your employer.

Living in Israel — practical settlement checklist

  • Read your employment agreement before signing.
  • Know your permitted employer, sector and work status.
  • Understand salary, payslip, lawful deductions, insurance and accommodation arrangements.
  • Keep copies of important employment information in a safe place.
  • Arrange a SIM/phone plan and learn how to contact your employer.
  • Learn your route to work and basic public-transport practice.
  • Know how to access medical care and emergency services.
  • Keep the contact details of your employer and relevant official services.
